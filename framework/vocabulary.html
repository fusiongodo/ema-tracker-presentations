Domain vocabulary

The terms that turn long explanations into one word. Ordered outside → in: from why people share data down to how a single symptom gets measured.

The app is an aggregated N-of-1 data commons that uses EMA for ecological validity and a crosswalk to harmonize user-defined check-ins with CDE-style instruments.

1Community

Why should anyone share their data?

Data commons V-data-commons

A shared, governed pool of data that many contribute to and many may use under common rules.

In our appEvery opted-in check-in flows into one pool that powers both the individual's insights and population-level analysis.

Related: positive externality, FAIR

Positive externality V-positive-externality

A benefit that an action creates for others without extra cost to the person acting (economics).

In our appThe core of the pitch: you track for yourself, and your data helps the next person at no extra effort.

Citizen science V-citizen-science

Research conducted with or by members of the public. In health: participatory research, where affected people help shape questions, data and interpretation.

In our appA DIY answer to underfunded ME/CFS research: patients generate evidence instead of waiting for it.

FAIR V-fair

Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable. The widely used principles for research data (Wilkinson et al., 2016).

In our appThe quality bar for the backend and export: data that researchers can actually reuse.

Related: interoperability

Interoperability V-interoperability

The ability of data from one system to be used by another without manual translation, through shared formats and shared variable definitions.

In our appAchieved through the crosswalk to standard instruments, not by forcing users onto fixed questionnaires.

Federated analysis V-federated-analysis

Analysis that runs where the data lives and returns only aggregate results, so raw data never leaves its source.

In our appA privacy option for the backend if central pooling of health data turns out to be too sensitive.

2Study design

How do self-experiments become evidence?

N-of-1 trial V-n-of-1

A trial with one participant who alternates between interventions in a planned sequence, with outcomes measured in each period.

In our appThe unit of work. Each user runs their own experiment.

Aggregated N-of-1 V-aggregated-n-of-1

A series of N-of-1 trials pooled to estimate both individual and population-level effects.

In our appThe app in two words: personal answers first, shared evidence as a by-product.

Crossover design V-crossover

Each participant receives every condition in sequence, so they serve as their own control.

Related: washout, carryover

Carryover V-carryover

An effect of a previous intervention that persists into the next period and contaminates its measurement.

Washout V-washout

A gap between intervention periods that lets carryover fade before the next condition starts.

Baseline (run-in) V-baseline

A period of measurement without intervention, establishing the person's normal level and variability.

In our appNeeded before any "did it help?" question can be answered.

Regression to the mean V-regression-to-the-mean

Extreme measurements tend to be followed by less extreme ones, regardless of any intervention.

In our appThe classic self-experiment trap: people start a treatment when they feel worst and would have improved anyway. A baseline guards against it.

Confounder V-confounder

A third factor that influences both the intervention and the outcome, creating a false or distorted association.

In our appEvents (infections, exertion, sleep) are logged partly to identify confounders.

Real-world evidence (RWE) V-rwe

Evidence derived from data collected outside controlled trials, in everyday life.

3Data collection

What comes in, and how clean is it?

EMA V-ema

Ecological Momentary Assessment: repeated real-time sampling of symptoms, behaviour and physiology in daily life. Origin: behavioural medicine.

In our appThe chosen term. Covers symptom check-ins, event logs and health-tracker data.

Related: ESM, sampling schedules

ESM V-esm

Experience Sampling Method: random prompts that sample momentary experience. Origin: psychology. Largely synonymous with EMA, narrower in scope.

Signal-, interval- and event-contingent sampling V-sampling-schedules

The three EMA schedules: prompted at random times (signal), at fixed times (interval), or entered when something happens (event).

In our appRandom check-ins are signal-contingent; therapy and event logs are event-contingent. Competitors (Self-E, StudyMe) only offer interval-contingent daily reminders.

Ecological validity V-ecological-validity

How well a measurement reflects real-life conditions rather than a lab or clinic setting.

Recall bias V-recall-bias

Systematic distortion when people report past states from memory, e.g. the worst or most recent day dominating a "last two weeks" rating.

In our appThe main problem EMA solves compared with retrospective questionnaires.

Respondent burden V-respondent-burden

The effort, time and energy a measurement costs the person answering.

In our appCritical in ME/CFS: answering itself costs energy. Design rule: simplicity beats perfect mapping.

MCAR, MAR, MNAR V-missingness

Missing completely at random, missing at random (explained by observed data), missing not at random (depends on the missing value itself).

In our appSkipped check-ins on bad days are MNAR: the gaps themselves carry information and must not be ignored in analysis.

Adherence and attrition V-attrition

Adherence: how consistently people follow the protocol. Attrition: how many drop out before the end. The "law of attrition" (Eysenbach, 2005) describes high dropout in digital health.

4Measurement

How does a feeling become a comparable number?

Latent construct V-latent-construct

A quantity that cannot be observed directly, such as fatigue, and is inferred from several observable items.

In our appThe middle nodes of the symptom tree (fatigue, brain fog, sleep, pain, daily functioning).

Facet V-facet

A distinguishable sub-dimension of a construct, e.g. physical vs. mental fatigue.

Granule Project term V-granule

The finest level of the symptom tree: exactly one sensation or capability. 68 granules in 12 sections, each mapped to instrument items.

Item and instrument V-item

An item is a single question with its response scale. An instrument is a validated set of items with a scoring rule.

PRO V-pro

Patient-reported outcome: a measurement reported directly by the patient, without interpretation by a clinician.

Crosswalk Verified V-crosswalk

A mapping that translates items or scores of one instrument into another. Established in psychometrics: the PROsetta Stone project produced crosswalk tables linking PROMIS to legacy instruments.

In our appExactly what the symptom tree is: user check-ins → granules → instrument items.

Related: harmonization, linking. Source: PROsetta R package

Linking and equating V-linking

Statistical methods (often based on item response theory) that place scores from different instruments on a common scale. Equating is the strict form for interchangeable tests.

In our appThe rigorous future version of the crosswalk, once enough data exists.

Harmonization V-harmonization

Making data collected with different instruments or definitions comparable for joint analysis.

Commensurable V-commensurable

Measurable on a common scale, so values can be compared or combined.

CDE Verified V-cde

Common Data Element: a standardized variable definition shared across studies. NINDS published ME/CFS CDEs in 2018, classifying instruments as core, supplemental-highly recommended, supplemental or exploratory.

In our appAnchor for the crosswalk. The DSQ, which the symptom tree already maps to, is a core CDE.

Source: NINDS ME/CFS CDE Highlight Summary (2018)

Ad hoc scale Project term V-ad-hoc-scale

A study-specific, self-built, non-validated scale. Not a scale defined by the user.

Construct validity V-construct-validity

Whether a measurement actually captures the construct it claims to measure.

5Disease context

What makes ME/CFS hard to measure?

PEM V-pem

Post-exertional malaise: worsening of symptoms after physical, cognitive or emotional exertion, often delayed by hours to days. The hallmark symptom of ME/CFS.

In our appThe delay makes it a lagged effect: analysis must look at exertion days before a symptom change, not the same day.

Crash V-crash

Patient term for a severe PEM episode.

Pacing and energy envelope V-pacing

Pacing: managing activity to avoid triggering PEM. Energy envelope: the range of activity a person can sustain without crashing.

In our appA natural outcome target: helping users find their envelope from their own data.
